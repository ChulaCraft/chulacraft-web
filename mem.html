Caveats

  • $/GB defaults to the cheapest retail price in nominal USD (not contract or average; retail lags contract). Use the Real USD toggle for inflation-adjusted values â constant 2024 dollars via US CPI-U annual averages (BLS).
  • The cheapest listing often tracks an end-of-life generation being cleared out, not the leading edge â the per-generation chart shows this.
  • These are cheapest listed prices over time (via Keepa), not confirmed sales. For the SSD data, obvious posting errors are removed â any month a drive is listed more than 60% below its own typical price (e.g. a $130 SSD shown at $4) is dropped.
  • The DRAM line splices two sources at mid-2024 (McCallum â Keepa); a small step there is expected, since Amazon's cheapest clearance can sit below McCallum's representative low.
  • Amazon's hard-drive category is dominated by renewed/recertified and relabelled ex-enterprise drives (about 95% of a plain search), which would otherwise set the "cheapest" price at roughly half the new price. The hard-disk line therefore tracks new consumer drives only, and only Amazon's own price: third-party "new" offers for the same drive often run 30â40% lower, consistent with recertified stock relisted as new. This matches McCallum buying new desktop drives from a retailer (NewEgg).
  • HBM figures are modeled estimates (cost share and spend), not measured prices.

Updates

DRAM, NAND and hard-disk $/GB refresh monthly from Keepa; HBM updates quarterly (Epoch AI). The McCallum backbone and HBM estimates are fixed. The downloadable CSV lists every point with its source.
